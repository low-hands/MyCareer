# Interview planning guidance

Use this reference only to build the internal interview plan. The workflow,
not the model, controls the exact number of primary questions and follow-ups.
Never expose the plan or answer guidance to the candidate in advance.

## Build a coverage map first

Classify potential coverage before selecting plan items:

1. role-critical requirements explicitly stated in the JD;
2. resume claims that strongly match, conflict with, or leave a gap against the JD;
3. applied reasoning: diagnosis, prioritization, design, execution, or trade-offs;
4. ownership and behavioral evidence relevant to the work;
5. motivation or career constraints, only when appropriate to the requested mode.

Rank candidates for inclusion by role relevance, evidence quality, and how much
the answer could change the practice assessment. Do not plan separate questions
that test the same fact with different wording.

When structured retros from earlier real interview rounds are supplied, use
their unresolved `difficulties` and `next_focus` as additional prioritization
signals only when they remain relevant to the current JD. They are the
candidate's recollection and self-assessment, not employer feedback. Do not
mechanically repeat remembered questions, infer an employer decision, or assume
the next interviewer will cover the same material. Do not expose the retro or
its hidden planning influence in question wording.

## Allocate a bounded plan

- There is no fixed ten-question requirement. Fill exactly the supplied
  `max_primary_questions` with the strongest available coverage.
- For `technical`, prioritize applied knowledge, problem solving, system design,
  and grounded project depth. Use a short introduction only when it creates a
  useful entry into later questions.
- For `role_specific`, prioritize realistic work scenarios, relevant professional
  judgment, and resume-grounded examples over generic textbook recall.
- For `behavioral`, vary the competency being tested: ownership, collaboration,
  conflict, failure, learning, influence, or judgment. Do not ask several generic
  STAR questions about the same project.
- For `hr`, cover material motivation, transition, expectations, constraints, and
  career direction without turning the session into a personality test.
- For `mixed`, normally combine role capability, resume/project depth, applied
  reasoning, and one behavioral or motivation area. When capacity is small,
  preserve the most role-critical JD item and the strongest grounded candidate
  evidence before adding breadth.

## Sequence for useful difficulty

Use a deliberate progression rather than random ordering:

1. establish a relevant baseline or concrete experience;
2. test one or more core capabilities;
3. deepen into reasoning, constraints, ownership, or trade-offs;
4. include a broader judgment or behavioral item when the mode calls for it;
5. end on a question that adds new evidence rather than repeating an earlier gap.

Difficulty is about cognitive demand, not obscure vocabulary:

- `introductory`: explain a relevant concept, experience, or straightforward choice;
- `intermediate`: apply knowledge to constraints, compare options, or diagnose a scenario;
- `advanced`: handle ambiguity, interacting trade-offs, failure modes, scale, or leadership judgment.

Infer difficulty from the role scope, JD wording, and demonstrated resume scope.
Do not use school prestige, employer brand, age, or other proxies as a difficulty
shortcut. If seniority or scale is unclear, record that limitation and avoid
inventing a level.

## Ground every item

- Use exact, short `jd_quotes` for requirements stated by the JD.
- When a resume claim motivates an item, provide aligned `resume_locators` and
  `resume_quotes`; never paraphrase an unsupported claim into a stronger one.
- A useful item may be grounded only in the JD, only in the resume, or both, but
  its `rationale` must explain why it earns scarce interview time.
- Treat an apparent JD/resume gap as a topic for learning or transfer reasoning,
  not proof that the candidate lacks the capability.
- Put material missing inputs, ambiguous seniority, absent resume evidence, and
  unsourced company-style assumptions in `limitations`.

## Quality gate

Before returning the plan, check that:

- the item count matches the requested bound and sequence numbers are contiguous;
- core JD coverage is not crowded out by generic questions;
- resume deep-dives point to supplied evidence rather than invented stories;
- question types and difficulty are varied enough to produce new evidence;
- each item has one clear focus and can support one concise primary question;
- no item depends on an unsupported claim about an employer's process or culture.
