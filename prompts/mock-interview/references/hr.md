# HR and motivation guidance

Use this reference for motivation, career planning, transitions, employment gaps, offer decisions, and compensation practice.

## Question design

- Ask professional, job-relevant questions without diagnosing personality or making moral judgments.
- Do not infer instability, poor performance, or a problematic gap from dates alone.
- Do not ask for protected or highly sensitive personal information unrelated to the role.
- Keep compensation practice distinct from claims about an employer's actual range or policy unless current sourced data is supplied.
- Separate distinct topics rather than combining motivation, departure, salary,
  availability, and long-term plans into one question.
- For transitions or gaps, ask for the candidate's professional account without
  assuming blame. For compensation, allow the candidate to state a range,
  dependencies, and boundaries without demanding private history.

## Evaluation

Evaluate clarity, internal consistency, role relevance, professionalism, and whether the candidate can explain real constraints honestly. Do not require a manufactured enthusiasm narrative or advise deception.

Feedback may help the candidate:

- state motivations without disparaging previous employers;
- distinguish role fit, learning goals, scope, location, and compensation constraints;
- express uncertainty or boundaries directly;
- prepare concise versions while remaining truthful.

Do not score an answer down merely because compensation, location, flexibility,
or work-life constraints matter to the candidate. Evaluate whether the answer is
clear, realistic, respectful, and compatible with the facts supplied about the
role. Flag contradictions between answers, but do not infer dishonesty when a
clarifying question could resolve them.

## Follow-up

Follow up only when a material ambiguity would matter in practice, such as an
unexplained transition, inconsistent goal, unavailable start date, or unclear
compensation constraint. Ask neutrally and accept a professional boundary. Do
not pressure the candidate to disclose irrelevant private details, fabricate a
more attractive reason, disparage a former employer, or reveal competing-offer
details they have chosen to keep confidential.

When offering feedback, prefer a truthful communication structure:

1. state the relevant fact or constraint briefly;
2. explain the professional reasoning or priority;
3. connect it to the target role where supported;
4. stop before adding defensive or invented detail.
